### Where is playing strength lost? (exact puzzles, depth probes, headline per-floor data, card chooser)

Within a fight, the product (10-29 net, two turns deep) plays close to the best that anything here can measure. Its remaining endgame error is about 0.3 HP per deeper decision. Every deeper-search variant, even an after-the-fact best of four, saves at most about 2 HP per elite. So the "~10% wrong picks at horizon >= 1" figure describes one-turn search on endgame tails. It is not a ceiling for the product, and it says nothing about mid-fight play, where the net leaf actually earns its lead. Runs are lost on the HP budget. 140 of 193 deaths over 1,000 headline sequences are at the boss, only 11 of those 140 entered with 70+ HP, and the observed curve says +10 HP at boss entry would roughly halve boss deaths. That HP goes at the two elites (about 31 HP per run). Elite HP loss tracks how long the fight lasts, which mostly reflects the deck's kill speed, and it barely moves with player strength: 19.1 to 15.7 HP per elite from the one-turn heuristic to the product, while clears go from 0.534 to 0.834. The deck comes from the sim card chooser, and the chooser is the weakest part left. Its trial fights are one turn deep even for the two-turn product. It never sees the first elite, and only 2 of its 10 picks see any elite. Its first three picks are decided almost entirely by 8 noisy fights against a random boss, because the easy next-floor part scores every option the same. Its picks flip on tiny leaf changes: 7.5-18% of first picks change between near-identical trial players. Three separate records show deaths moving between the boss and the second elite when picks change, which is what a chooser that cannot see elites would produce. My conclusion: the single change most likely to raise win rate is a less noisy, elite-aware card chooser (more shared-seed trial fights, the next elite and the sequence's own boss in its horizon). This is an inference from indirect evidence, not a measured result. The cheapest check is whether its picks are stable across trial-fight seeds.

- chooser-pick-stability: Re-score the existing headline chooser decisions (start with the floor-0 to floor-2 offers of 100-200 sequences from the one-turn control) with the same sim chooser, but 5 different trial-fight seed bases. Count how often the pick changes, and the score gap between the top two options. (CPU: About 0.1-0.3 thread-h (100-200 sequences x 4 options x 16 one-turn fights x 5 seeds))
- elite-aware-chooser: Give the sim chooser the next elite ahead (at the HP that fight would start at) and the sequence's own boss as horizon parts, with 16-32 shared-seed fights per part. Read it one turn deep on the headline (own picks, fixed n 500, against yardstick/one-turn-controls), then on the pack-offers setting. (CPU: About 3-6 thread-h per headline arm (the chooser's fights grow 2-4x))
- stop-fight-depth-on-official: Open no more lanes on deeper or variant fight search (three turns, rollouts, more hands, tie-breaks) on official content. Keep the depth family closed. (CPU: 0)
- two-turn-trial-player-at-boss-part: For the product only, let the chooser's boss part play two turns deep, so cards are valued by how the actual player uses them. (CPU: High: two-turn boss games cost about 41 thread-s each (measurement/boss-cost@two-turn), so 8 boss fights x 4 options x 1)
- lagavulin-horizon-probe: A three-turn read on the 174 floor-9 Lagavulin fights only (from two4-elite-all), outcome-blind, paired against two-turn play. (CPU: About 15 thread-h at about 8 GB (three turns cost about 5 thread-min per elite fight))

### Goal 1: generalisation and custom content (unseen cards, powers, enemies, a new character, mechanic or entity)

What the data shows: the design generalises through the simulator, not through the net's encoding. Any effect the engine can apply inside the search horizon is already in the afterstate, so it costs nothing zero-shot, even when the net cannot read the atom at all. 19 of 22 Act 1 pack cards are level, including all six new-atom cards, and the product net reads only 8 of today's 19 triggers and 27 of 48 effect columns. Every measured zero-shot cost is the same kind of thing: value that keeps paying after the leaf (a rate per future turn). Examples are Demon Form's later ticks, Poison, Ritual 2, enemy Strength or Weak left in the leaf state, and reactive enemy powers. The net prices these badly even for known modifiers. The effect-primitive encoding's edge over an id-only net is real but small, and it shrinks with search depth and with data: +0.059 wins one turn deep and +0.017 (0.012) two turns deep on generated power cards, and the Silent warm-start edge is gone after 500 games. No experiment has shown meta-generalisation, meaning that training on a space of generated mechanics teaches the net to price unseen ones; the tests so far were too insensitive or negative. My own recompute on the generated-enemies bench shows novel enemy content mostly makes the fight harder for every player: the heuristic's and the net's per-cell costs correlate 0.87. The random-world bench agrees: the net's cost beyond the heuristic stays flat as mechanics stack. Everything measured sits one step from official content and covers only power cards and enemy start powers. There is no generator for attacks, skills, whole monsters or a character, and no reference for good play on novel content except bursts. On custom content by data, the 10-spec engine series is essentially built (specs 1-9 and registry stages 1-4), so asking whether it is gold-plating is moot. What remains is small for a character (one Rust enum plus the relic kind). For a mechanic it is structural: the closed atom vocabulary means every new mechanic is engine code, about one block per two novel cards in the tail. My interpretation: goal 1's real levers are (a) how fast mechanics enter the simulator and (b) a content-agnostic way to price persistent state the search can't play out. More net vocabulary or more per-mechanic distance-1 benches are not levers.

- Wholesale random worlds: a distance curve with an in-world reference: Write two generators: attacks and skills from every atom (Damage, Block, Draw, Apply, AddCard, expressions, picks), priced by the measured win-rate change of a cost-0 copy under search (not by hook_rates); and whole monsters (HP, 2-4 moves from atoms, pattern rows, an optional generated power). Then build worlds with a share d of the deck cards and enemy kinds generated, d in {0, 0.25, 0.5, 1}, ab ... (CPU: Generators: code, no CPU. Zero-shot sweep: about 4 d x 20 worlds x 200 fights x 4 players, roughly 64k one-turn games at)

### Prior art and the Stockfish analogy (fishtest/SPRT, NNUE, AlphaZero/MuZero, TD-Gammon/GNU Backgammon, poker AIVAT, Hearthstone/StS bots)

Ingredient by ingredient, sts-for-ai already has most of what fishtest is made of. It has an SPRT on paired games (scripts/ladder.py with alpha = beta = 0.05; scripts/analysis/gauntlet_protocol.py on end-HP), pinned binaries, a job queue, and a cheap tier plus an expensive tier (one turn and two turns deep, like fishtest's short and long time controls). So the 11-09 review's point that the "measurement multiplier" is missing is only half right. At one turn deep, a fishtest-style test at H1 = 0.5 HP already costs about 3 thread-hours. What fishtest relies on and this project lacks is a cheap tier that is the same player as the product. A Stockfish game at a short time control is the same engine as at the long one, so gains mostly carry over. Here, leaf gains measured one turn deep reach the two-turn product at only 0.2-0.46x (cause/leaf-gain-third). The product tier costs about 55x more per sequence (82.5 against 1.5 thread-s). The tight2 proxy, a different task, even ranked nets in the wrong order. The search curve also tells us which prior art fits. One turn to two turns gave +0.154 clears, and three turns did not move clears. That is backgammon's shape (GNU Backgammon plays 2-ply), not chess's, so the "deeper search on Stockfish's pattern" route is mostly used up. The backgammon route (a better evaluator trained at scale, rollouts, luck-corrected measurement) and the run-level value carry the remaining gains. Two measurement gaps that the earlier reviews did not name show up in the data. First, training-seed luck is as large as the effects being chased: twin nets from the same recipe differ by +3.62 HP (se 0.97) and +0.076 clears on the pack setting, and an SPRT over sequences cannot remove this. Second, 288 reads were made on one sequence seed, 150 of them on sequences 0-499; fishtest avoids reusing a small book like this. Across fishtest (non-regression bounds of [0, 2] Elo) and AlphaZero (which dropped AlphaGo Zero's 55% gate), dominant systems improve by stacking many steps that are each too small to detect. The project's rules (the minimum-detectable-effect gate, the plateau stop, the 3x rule) block that loop by design. That is right for research questions and wrong for an improvement loop. ### Throughput and process: where wall-clock and CPU time went from 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-10, and what limits useful experiments per day

The records do not support "machine speed is the bottleneck". Over 184 hours since 2026-11-03, the job runner's jobs reserved 3.7 threads on average, nothing ran for 26% of the time, and 8 or more threads were in use for only 3% of it. Two things limit the machine. First, memory: memory was the most common reason a job waited (212 waits, against 121 for the two heavy slots and 34 for threads). Second, the decision loop is serial: a lane queues one step, waits a median 1.3 h for it to start, the job runs, a session wakes (median 8 min, mean 47 min) and reads the result, and only then is the next job queued. Whenever sessions stop (on 11-09, or with the runner dead), the CPU goes idle. Where the CPU did go: evaluation took 54% (mutate-monsters 30%, gauntlet 24%), test-full 15%, and learning only 10.5%. Inside a learn, about half the time is torch training on the CPU, and that training is slow because of how its data is stored, not because of the maths. The 8.3 GB memory-mapped feature cache is 2-5% non-zero, almost all small integers, so it does not fit in the RAM that is free. Identical epochs vary 3-7x in time (111 to 880 s) depending on what else runs. The same mapped pages inflate the learn's measured peak, which then blocks other jobs on memory. The cheapest large wins are: a sparse feature cache (much faster learns, and memory freed for a second heavy job), running more heavy jobs at once, filler work that fills idle cores instead of an empty queue, queuing whole decision trees instead of one step per session, sequential stopping (stop a read once its verdict is decided) in more reads, and tiered tests.

### Measurement and evaluation: can the yardstick detect progress toward a "Stockfish-level" StS AI?

The brief is out of date in two ways. The tight-fight gate (Amendment 5) was retired as a goal-2 ruler on 2026-11-02. Today's ruler is the 11-fight headline gauntlet (HP carried between fights): an SPRT on paired end-HP (H1 +1.5 HP) with a guard on clears. And the 0.006-0.0075 training-seed sd is marked in the kb as one shared-draw group. The general sd is 0.010 train / 0.013 held-out. The current ruler is good at one job: seeing large search-time and run-layer changes. Two-turn search shows up at z 7-8 in 500 sequences, and nearly every real gain since 10-29 is that kind. It cannot see the effect sizes that training and recipe work produce. Between nets, paired end-HP sd is about 20 HP, so the SPRT needs about 940 sequences on average to accept a true +1.5 HP, against a 500 cap. A 0.005 change in clears needs about 95k sequences. On top of that, training-seed noise means about 30 seeds per arm for a 0.5 HP recipe effect. Tight fights also measure the wrong regime: their cells start at a median 5-6 HP and 73% are hallway fights. In the headline, only 0.7-1.2% of fights start at 15 HP or less, and hallway fights cause only 7-11% of deaths. Deaths come mostly at bosses entered with 30-60 HP. Inside the headline, the boss fight's luck is 45-59% of the variance in clears. End-HP also counts HP left after the boss, which the real game heals at the act transition; that part is 30-55% of small end-HP edges. The structural gap is that every number is relative to our own agents on our own curated arena. Nothing anchors it to the field (SpireBench, sts-ironclad-agent), and nothing measures how far the product sits from the best possible play. So "Stockfish-level" cannot be detected by any statistic of the current ruler, however well tuned. The first new metric to adopt: clears corrected for chance luck with a value function (AIVAT), aimed first at boss and elite fights. It keeps exactly the quantity that matters (clears), it is unbiased by construction, and it removes the dominant noise source. Sharper reads come right after it: a difficulty knob so the arena does not saturate as relics arrive, and a fixed ladder of reference agents to anchor it.
