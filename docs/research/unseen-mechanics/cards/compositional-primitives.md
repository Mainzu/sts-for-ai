# Compositional primitives: scout cards

Angle: systematic and compositional generalisation in RL and planning (modular
networks, relational and factored value functions, BabyAI-style benchmarks,
object-centric world models, lifted planning).

Eight papers opened, all through their arXiv HTML pages (read through
WebFetch, which summarises long pages). Citation counts come from OpenAlex by DOI.

### Discovering modular solutions that generalize compositionally (2023)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15001v2 (abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15001) · **Venue:** arXiv (v2, Mar 2024); the page shows no venue · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex, arXiv record only) · **Read:** full text (first 100,000 of 269,090 characters)
- **Problem:** Can modular networks find hidden compositional structure in multi-task data and generalise to module combinations they never saw together?
- **Method:** A hypernetwork builds the first-layer weights as W = sum over m of z(m) times Theta(m). Each task has a sparse latent z with at most K nonzero entries, so the task picks and mixes shared modules. For a new task, the latent is inferred from support demonstrations by inner-loop gradient descent (no second-order gradients). Baselines are ANIL and MAML.
- **Assumes:** Supervised regression with MSE in the teacher-student and Hyperteacher settings, with finite data per task. Tasks are sparse combinations of 1 to K of M modules. The theorem uses infinite data and a linear teacher and student. RL appears only in a compositional-preference grid world (up to K=3 of M=8 preference modules, action-value from optimal trajectories) and a goal grid world (maze, target object, target action, goal quadrant). Inputs for a new task are support demonstrations.
- **Claims:** Theorem (linear case): zero loss on N = dim(span(Z_k)) + 1 i.i.d. task samples per family suffices, almost surely. Hyperteacher: hypernetworks reach high out-of-distribution accuracy only with compositional support; ANIL and MAML fail out of distribution; disconnected support hurts. Exact numbers are in figures only, not in the text the fetch returned. Seeds: 3, with standard error.
- **Evaluation:** Hyperteacher (M modules, held-out K-combinations as the out-of-distribution set); compositional-preference grid world; compositional-goal grid world (four factors).
- **Code:** https://github.com/smonsays/modular-hyperteacher (license not checked)
- **Fit:** partial. It fits new combinations of known modules and adapts to a new task by inferring a small latent from a few demonstrations, with no retraining. It breaks on disconnected training support and needs many training families. Tested only on toy regression and grid worlds.

### Learning Dynamic Attribute-factored World Models for Efficient Multi-object Reinforcement Learning (2023)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2307.09205 · **Venue:** arXiv 2307.09205 (a NeurIPS 2023 poster page appears in search results; the fetched text does not state the venue) · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex, UvA-DARE record) · **Read:** full text (first 100,000 of 143,212 characters)
- **Problem:** Multi-object RL agents must generalise to unseen object counts, unseen attribute combinations and tasks built from skills learned earlier.
- **Method:** DAFT-MDP. Each object class gets a template graph (a dynamic Bayesian network) over its own attributes, latent parameters and the action. Pairs of classes get interaction-pattern graphs. Soft attention binds the action to the one object it affects. A dynamic interaction graph (NRI style) infers which objects interact at each step. The policy is learned in imagination (MPC or PPO). For a new environment, latent parameters and the interaction graph are inferred from a few trajectories, with no policy retraining.
- **Assumes:** Object-centric observations, either symbolic or from pretrained Slot Attention or AIR. A known, fixed class system. Sparse interactions in time and in attributes. One object affected per action. Latent parameters constant per object. Determinism is not stated.
- **Claims:** Push and Switch, success over 3 seeds. 3-Push+3-Switch (S+O): DAFT-RL 0.805 plus or minus 0.024, NCS 0.751 plus or minus 0.024, STOVE 0.672. 2-Push+2-Switch (L+S): DAFT-RL 0.793 plus or minus 0.026, LRN 0.634 plus or minus 0.045. Counter-case: on 2-Push+2-Switch (S), NCS is higher at 0.912 plus or minus 0.022 against DAFT-RL 0.881 plus or minus 0.038. Spriteworld Object Goal (unseen object numbers, colours, shapes): DAFT-RL (SA) 0.897 plus or minus 0.034, NCS 0.847 plus or minus 0.022. Block stacking, 8 blocks: DAFT-RL (AIR) 0.571 plus or minus 0.039, DAFT-RL (SA) 0.506 plus or minus 0.083.
- **Evaluation:** Push and Switch (symbolic, OpenAI Fetch based; held-out skill combinations S, more objects O, changed latent parameters L). Spriteworld (unseen object numbers and colour or shape combinations). Block stacking (unseen block counts and mass combinations).
- **Code:** none found in the fetched text. The CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 footer applies to the paper, not to code.
- **Fit:** partial. It fits transfer without retraining, through per-object latent inference and attribute-level factoring. It breaks on a new object class or a new attribute kind, since the class system is fixed and known. It needs object-centric inputs. The CPU cost of the GRU-based models is not stated.

### Inductive Biases for Zero-shot Systematic Generalization in Language-informed Reinforcement Learning (2025)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15270 · **Venue:** arXiv 2501.15270 (submitted Jan 2025); the fetched page shows no venue. OpenAlex lists it under "Machine Learning" (2025) · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text (first 100,000 of 104,244 characters)
- **Problem:** Zero-shot systematic generalisation to unseen combinations of instruction components in BabyAI.
- **Method:** ICMO. Neural Production Systems turn entity slots into outputs through attention-based rule and context selection. An LSTM memory is fed back into rule and context selection. The instruction embedding enters the memory input (IC-M), which the ablation found best. The paper argues that language grounded in memory gates the modules, so compositional knowledge transfers. This is an argument backed by ablations, not a proof.
- **Assumes:** BabyAI symbolic 7x7x3 partial observations. PPO with Adam at 1e-4, 16 parallel environments, LSTM hidden size 1024, backprop through 20 steps. Training budgets of 8M to 30M frames per level. Two seeds. A systematic split by held-out instruction strings.
- **Claims:** Average over six levels, test split: ICMO success 0.97 plus or minus 0.01, mean reward 0.85 plus or minus 0.01, generalisation gap 0.01 plus or minus 0.01. Baselines: CNN-GRU success 0.74 plus or minus 0.06, AttentionFusion 0.76 plus or minus 0.08, FiLM-BabyAI 0.09 plus or minus 0.05. Per level, ICMO test success is 1.00 on ActionObjDoor, 1.00 on GoToSeq, 0.98 on PickupLoc, 0.99 on PutNextLocal, 1.00 on OpenDoorsOrder, 0.86 on Synth.
- **Evaluation:** BabyAI levels ActionObjDoor, GoToSeq, PickupLoc, PutNextLocal, OpenDoorsOrder, Synth. Held-out object combinations include "red box", "green ball", "purple key", "yellow box", "blue ball", "grey key". 512 test episodes per level.
- **Code:** https://github.com/nhashemi202/ICMO.git · licence not stated on the page
- **Fit:** partial. The slot-based rules are a modular design that generalises to unseen instruction combinations. But the inputs are symbolic grid views, not card text. Training uses 8M to 30M PPO frames with a 1024-unit LSTM, and the CPU cost is not stated.

### Zero-Shot Compositional Policy Learning via Language Grounding (2020)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2004.07200 (abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07200) · **Venue:** arXiv (v2 revised 17 Apr 2023); the page names no venue · **Cited by:** 3 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Language-guided RL and imitation agents overfit to the environments they train on. Tile colour and tile property pairs seen at test time are unseen in training. The text describes each pairing.
- **Method:** Multi-modal attention fusion (MMA). Attention over CNN features gives a probability over the k description sentences plus a "no description" slot. The weighted sentence embeddings are concatenated with the scene features at each grid cell, so each cell is tied to one description. Baselines are concat fusion and FiLM.
- **Assumes:** MiniGrid and BabyAI 7x7 views with a two-layer CNN. PPO with a two-layer, width-64 actor-critic, Adam at 1e-4. RL frames: 10M for GoToRedBall-v1, 100M for PickupLoc and PutNextLocal, 200M for the rest. Imitation learning uses 1M demonstrations. Six tile properties: trap, slippery, flipLeftRight, flipUpDown, sticky, magic.
- **Claims:** Success, train / test. PutNextLocal (RL): baseline 0.636 / 0.654, FiLM 0.766 / 0.723, MMA 0.834 / 0.790. GoToRedBall-v2 (RL): FiLM 0.707 / 0.586, MMA 0.846 / 0.743. Per the authors, MMA beats FiLM by 29.5% on PickupLoc and 21.8% on GoToRedBall-v2, averaged over RL and IL. Text ablation on PutNextLocal (RL), all texts: MMA 0.984 / 0.989.
- **Evaluation:** BabyAI++, with held-out colour and property pairs. For GoToRedBall-v1 and v2, GoToObj and GoTo-v2, the held-out pairs are (blue, sticky), (blue, magic), (green, trap), (green, flipUpDown), (grey, flipLeftRight) and (grey, slippery). For PutNextLocal, GoTo-v1 and PickupLoc, they are (blue, slippery) and (green, trap).
- **Code:** none found in the page
- **Fit:** partial. The benchmark is a close analogue of new combinations of known primitives: colour times property. The agent is a large PPO CNN that needs 10M to 200M frames, which breaks the CPU and small-net constraints. A new tile property would be new engine code.

### Relational Deep Reinforcement Learning (2018)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01830 (html: https://arxiv.org/html/1806.01830) · **Venue:** arXiv (v2, 28 Jun 2018); the page lists no peer-reviewed venue · **Cited by:** 154 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Deep RL agents are sample-inefficient and overfit to small task changes. The paper adds a relational inductive bias.
- **Method:** A CNN makes k feature maps over an n x n grid, and each cell's x and y coordinates are appended. Multi-head dot-product self-attention then computes pairwise interactions among the n-squared entities. Each block has a residual connection, layer norm and an MLP. Blocks can be shared (recurrent) or deep. Max-pooling and a small MLP produce the policy and value.
- **Assumes:** Pixel observations of Box-World (12x12 grid, 20 keys and 20 locks per level, training solution lengths 1 to 4, 0 to 4 distractor branches). IMPALA-style training with 100 actors and batches of 32. RMSprop at 1e-5 to 2e-4. 2 to 4 attention heads. StarCraft II runs for 10 billion steps.
- **Claims:** Box-World, training levels: relational agent above 98%, controls below 75%. Zero-shot longer solutions (6, 8, 10 boxes): relational above 88% in all three, control 5% on 6 boxes and 0% on 8 and 10. New key-lock combinations never needed in training: relational 97%, control 13%. StarCraft II, mini-game Defeat Roaches: relational 303, control 295, grandmaster 215.
- **Evaluation:** Box-World (generated levels), with the held-out key-lock and longer-chain tests above. SC2LE mini-games, with a marine-count generalisation test on Collect Mineral Shards (trained at 2 marines, tested at 1 to 5 and 10).
- **Code:** none found. The paper says Box-World "will be made publicly available", with no link or licence.
- **Fit:** partial. It is the closest match to new combinations of known entity types and relations in a pooled-entity network, and the key-lock result is a clear number. It breaks the CPU constraint at StarCraft scale and uses pixels. The attention-over-entities idea overlaps the Set Transformer card already done.

### Learning Generalized Policies Without Supervision Using GNNs (2022)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2205.06002 · **Venue:** KR 2022 (per OpenAlex) · **Cited by:** 16 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Learn a value function over lifted STRIPS states that generalises from small instances to larger ones, then derive a greedy policy from it. No optimal labels are used.
- **Method:** A GNN with one message MLP per predicate and a shared object-update MLP. The readout sums per-object outputs and passes the result through a second MLP. Embedding size 64, 30 layers, smooth-max aggregation (alpha 8). The loss enforces V(s) >= 1 + min V(successors) and keeps V between V* and 2V*. The greedy policy picks the successor with the lowest V, with cycle avoidance as an option.
- **Assumes:** Discrete, deterministic, unit-cost actions, no dead ends. Inputs are the objects and the true ground atoms of each state. Training on small instances with breadth-first states, capped at 40,000. Training ran up to 12 hours on NVIDIA A100 GPUs, five models per domain.
- **Claims:** Test coverage with the L1 loss, deterministic greedy, over 269 instances: 90% with cycle avoidance, 77% without. Per domain with cycle avoidance: Blocks 100%, Delivery 100%, Gripper 100%, Logistics 60%, Miconic 100%, Reward 73%, Spanner* 73%, Visitall 100%. Plan-length ratio, solved instances: Miconic 1.0052, Blocks 1.0427, Visitall 1.0183.
- **Evaluation:** Eight classical domains with the test counts Blocks 20, Delivery 15, Gripper 16, Logistics 28, Miconic 120, Reward 15, Spanner* 41, Visitall 14. Data and code are in the Zenodo archive.
- **Code:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6511809 (Zenodo record, licence not stated on the page)
- **Fit:** partial. A value function over predicates that generalises to larger object counts without relabelling is the pattern being sought. It breaks the CPU and small-net constraints: 30 layers, embedding 64, GPU training for hours. It covers discrete planning only.

### Relational GNNs Cannot Learn C2 Features for Planning (2025)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11721v1 · **Venue:** arXiv 2506.11721 (June 2025); no peer-reviewed venue shown · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Can relational GNN value functions represent the counting and two-variable (C2) features that some optimal planning values need?
- **Method:** A theory and toy argument. Two instances over one binary predicate with a goal feature phi_goalNotAchieved2 give the same R-GNN output. Random-weight test: 100,000 configurations, embedding 64, sum aggregation, 30 layers. Training test: Adam at 2e-4 for 10,000 steps. The paper proposes PLOI graphs (objects as nodes, binary predicates as labelled edges, reversed edges included) as an alternative, with no experiments.
- **Assumes:** STRIPS planning with predicates of arity at most two. Higher arity is compiled to binary, which loses information. Deterministic actions. Goal copies (p_G predicates).
- **Claims:** Random initialisation: the relative difference between outputs on the two instances stayed under 1% of the maximum output in all 100,000 trials (the paper notes numerical precision limits this). Training: the loss never went below 1, the mean of targets 2 and 0, the best achievable if the inputs look identical. No benchmark numbers; PLOI has no results.
- **Evaluation:** One toy counterexample (objects a and b; instances I1 and I2).
- **Code:** R-GNN repository simon-stahlberg/relational-neural-network-python at commit dd70a46 (licence not stated on the page)
- **Fit:** partial. It is a warning, not a method. A sum-pooled relational value net may not represent pair or count features. That matters if effect features need counting, such as "how many cards of kind X". It has no evidence on game-like data.

### One Policy to Control Them All: Shared Modular Policies for Agent-Agnostic Control (2020)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2007.04976 (abs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04976) · **Venue:** ICML 2020 (per the ICML poster page in search results; the fetched page shows no venue) · **Cited by:** 56 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text, but the generalisation results are in figures
- **Problem:** One policy should control agents with different morphologies, including unseen ones, instead of one controller per robot.
- **Method:** One identical module at every actuator, with parameters shared across all limbs of all agents. Each module sees only its limb's local state. Neighbouring modules exchange 32-dimensional learned messages along the limb tree, bottom-up, top-down, or both-way. Children's messages are combined by sum, average or max, and the graph is padded to the largest child count. Training uses TD3 with Adam at 4e-4.
- **Assumes:** Continuous control in planar Gym MuJoCo tasks. The morphology is a tree of limbs. Module inputs are position, velocities, 3D rotation and normalised joint range. 1,000-step episodes. Four seeds. 20% of variants held out for zero-shot tests.
- **Claims:** Root-choice training reward at 1M steps, walker, mean plus or minus std over 4 seeds: both-way with left-foot root 3709.87 plus or minus 580.87; torso root 3215.04 plus or minus 447.82; monolithic baseline 3592.70 plus or minus 111.13. Zero-shot results on held-out morphologies are given only as figures. The text says both-way message passing generalises best there.
- **Evaluation:** walker++ (12 variants), humanoid++ (8), cheetah++ (15), walker-hopper++ (12 walkers plus 3 hoppers), and a combined set. 20% of variants held out.
- **Code:** none linked; project page https://huangwl18.github.io/modular-rl/ has videos only
- **Fit:** partial. It shows one small shared module reused over a variable-size graph of parts, with unseen variants, which maps onto per-card or per-power modules. It breaks on discrete rules: it is continuous locomotion, the graph topology must be known, and the generalisation numbers are in figures.
