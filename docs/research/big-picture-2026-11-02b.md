## Diagnosis

Where the project stands. Goal 2: the product player (burst8hp2 net, two turns deep, @hpe0.01) clears 0.676 of the 500-sequence Act-1 gauntlet. The one-turn hand heuristic clears 0.596, so the product is +0.08 above a hand-written baseline in a simplified Act 1 that has no map, potions, shops, events, upgrades or relics beyond Burning Blood. Goal 1 has no standing headline number at all. All six analyses agree on what moved strength. Every lasting combat gain came from changing what search optimises or how far it looks: the HP bonus +0.168 clears, the end-HP fix +0.036, depth +0.060. The one run-layer lever tried, card rewards from the starter deck, beats all of them: +0.184 for the sim chooser over random picks and +0.364 for random picks over skipping. Training-side work (labels, curriculum, recipes, data size, coverage, generated content, distillation, ensembles) came out at effects of 0 to 0.02, below its own noise floor. The two training changes promoted on proxies (burst8, burst8hp2) were reversed at run level by an older net, on a single seed.

The underlying pattern is that the project optimises one component against a proxy whose unit differs from the game's unit, then builds the right measurement only after the proxy has misled it. The learned leaf was trained toward fight outcomes. It was selected on tight2, which is calibrated at a median start HP of 5-6, so it tests lethal racing, the one regime where HP is worth nothing. It was then promoted on run-HP, a proxy that drops a stage of the outcome. Meanwhile the real objective is clearing the run. Every big win was a hand repair of that unit mismatch: pricing HP, fixing the floored finish score, choosing the deck. The analyses mostly see this as separate symptoms: yardstick drift, underpowered arms, a blind tight2, objective fixes winning.

Two things are holding the project back. (1) Most of the CPU went to the part of the system with the least leverage, the fight-level leaf. That is about 164 measured thread-hours of null or redundant training arms in 66 hours, plus roughly 150-250 before the job runner existed. Those arms were read on rulers that could not see them. (2) The goal-2 test bed leaves out most of the run decisions that decide real runs: path and elite avoidance, rest heal vs upgrade, removal, potions, relics. The evidence that losses are decided before the fatal fight is strong. Boss win at matched entry HP is the same for every player we have (Hexaghost at 60 HP: 0.55-0.61). Elite HP loss is flat at 26-30 per sequence across players. Rollouts rescue 7 of 117 fatal fights. Card choice, by contrast, lifts boss win given arrival from about 0.70 to 0.886. So the main remaining goal-2 headroom is in the run layer, plus whatever an unmeasured stronger combat player could add. More leaf training is not where it is.

Goal 1 follows the same pattern. What generalises to unseen content is the simulator, through depth: +0.274 on generated turn-start powers in a pre-registered replication, and +0.32 on Silent Poison elites. The effects representation helps only zero-shot and close to the training content. Per-mechanic feature columns trade one mechanic against another. The tested content is a narrow corner: power cards only, with no generated attacks, skills or monsters.

The machine is less of a bottleneck than the operating notes assume. Jobs booked about half the machine's threads over the job-runner era, falling to 6 threads overnight on 11-01/02. Two-turn search needs about 1.1 GB per thread and one-turn about 0.2-0.3 GB, so memory caps two-turn jobs while one-turn jobs leave cores idle. The highest-value moves right now are to stop feeding the leaf-training family, put the freed CPU into one-turn screens and the run layer, and settle the two unmeasured quantities every verdict depends on: run-level training-seed noise, and combat headroom against a stronger reference player.

## Stop doing

- Stop running training-recipe arms (labels, curriculum, bursts, data size, coverage, distillation, generated-content share, ensembles, per-mechanic feature columns such as rates and thresholds) as goal-2 levers. Their effects sat at |z| 1.2-1.7, and the ones that passed reversed at run level. A training arm runs only if it names a measured bias and states an expected run-level effect above the run-level MDE, which needs the seed sd first.
- Stop using tight2, run-HP, the ladder or bench regret to promote or kill anything for goal 2. tight2 keeps one job: the goal-1 check that held-out cards don't get worse. Gate goal 2 only on the gauntlet protocol.
- Stop reading the survival-score arms (jobs/pending/0059l8a, 0059l8b, 6 GB and 6 threads each) on ladder runhp. Park them until the run-level seed sd is known. If they run, read them on the one-turn gauntlet against net1029-hpb0.01.json.
- Stop running paired reads at a fixed n unless an effect size is the goal. Every pending gauntlet screen gets --against, and the same SPRT goes into depth_paired, ladder and bench reads.
- Stop spending boss-specific combat effort (deeper boss search, boss leaf fixes, hand prices like @esp) on goal 2 until a stronger reference player shows that headroom exists at matched HP. At matched HP, boss win doesn't move with any play change, and the hand Strength price lost -0.020.
- Stop using the exact-puzzle accuracy (about 10% wrong picks) as a ceiling estimate. 77% of its puzzles are at horizon 0, and all of them are endgame tails.
- Stop building engine-next generality just to keep old nets and recorded games bit-identical. Relax the rule going forward to 'old nets load, then get fine-tuned'. A burst fine-tune takes about 10 minutes and a retrain about an hour.
- Stop capping one-turn jobs at 3 threads and 2 slots. Memory, not the slot count, should decide how many jobs run.
