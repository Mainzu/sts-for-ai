### Improving Hearthstone AI by Combining MCTS and Supervised Learning Algorithms (2018)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04794 (full text: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.04794) · **Venue:** IEEE CIG 2018, pp. 445-452 (per arXiv page) · **Cited by:** 55 (OpenAlex W2885766801) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Make MCTS for Hearthstone stronger and cheaper under hidden information and randomness, by learning to evaluate game states.
- **Method:** Custom MCTS for hidden-info and random games. A 750-element state vector (low-level features for both players, minions, hand, turn number, and skip-gram word2vec card embeddings of size 10 trained on card text) feeds 3 dense layers (256, 128, 64, tanh) with two softmax outputs that classify the winner. At leaves, random simulations stop after a player's last move in a turn (not before 20 steps), and the tree is updated with the network's win probability.
- **Assumes:** Authors' own C++ simulator with 483 implemented cards. Two fixed decks (ZooWarlock, CubeWarlock), fixed seats. Time budget per move 0.5 s or 1.0 s (no iteration counts). Training data generated by a "cheater" MCTS that sees hidden information. Iterative training: 3,000 games per iteration, 1M-sample FIFO buffer.
- **Claims:** Win rate vs. plain MCTS at 0.5 s/move: baseline P1 73.5%, P2 26.5%; heuristic bots as P1 78.4%, 82.0%, 79.0%; as P2 up to 56.1%. At 1.0 s/move: heuristic bots as P1 71.6% to 76.0%, as P2 about 47% to 56%. Value network accuracy 0.775 (P1) and 0.794 (P2) when trained on one deck type. Preliminary training: 3.5M samples from 400 decks, validation accuracy 0.76. Computation reduction is claimed in the abstract but not quantified in the body. The paper does not test unseen cards.
- **Evaluation:** 500 games per configuration in Tables I and II (two decks, vs. plain MCTS and random). Glicko-2 ranking over 50,000+ matches among 64 bot versions. Small human match set (about 50% vs. Regular players, 43% vs. Legend players, per the table as extracted).
- **Code:** none found (Papers With Code lists "no code implementations"; GitHub not checked)
- **Fit:** partial. It is the closest match to "learned leaf evaluator inside search" in a stochastic card game. It breaks on generalisation: only two decks, unseen cards not tested. Its data generation uses hidden-information cheating (training-time privilege), which may conflict with the no-peeking constraint if that covers training.

### Mastering Strategy Card Game (Hearthstone) with Improved Techniques (2023)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05197 (full text: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.05197) · **Venue:** IEEE CoG 2023 (DOI 10.1109/cog57401.2023.10333220; arXiv comment says "cog2023 full") · **Cited by:** 16 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Learn a strong Hearthstone agent end-to-end, covering deck building and battle, with no search at play time.
- **Method:** One policy network for deck-building and battle with shared card and hero embeddings. Training uses optimistic smooth fictitious play over historical policies, with V-Trace and UPGO policy gradients, later a PPO-style objective. Actions are auto-regressive (type, target) with masks. Value is estimated from intermediate features.
- **Assumes:** Hearthbreaker-based environment with three heroes (Mage, Warrior, Hunter), approximating the April 2015 Blackrock Mountain version. Pool of 350+ cards, about 270+ available per hero in deck-building. Discrete turns. Training uses asymmetric "cheat" (opponent's first n chosen cards visible). Hardware not checked.
- **Claims:** Machine vs. machine (2 x 3 x 3 x 2500 matches per pairing): b4 vs. b0 73.6%; c5 vs. b0 80.2%; c5 vs. b4 55.5%. Discount gamma 1.0 gave about +7% vs. b0. Machine vs. human: a top-10 (China league) streamer lost 3 of 15 games across four Conquest Bo5 tournaments (3:0, 3:0, 3:1, 3:2 for the bots).
- **Evaluation:** Hearthbreaker-based self-play and vs. b0 (COG 2022 LoCM double-championship baseline), plus one human Conquest series.
- **Code:** none linked by the paper. It cites the open-source Hearthbreaker (https://github.com/danielyule/hearthbreaker) as its base.
- **Fit:** partial. Learns a value and policy end-to-end, with no search at play time, so it does not test search with a learned evaluator. The paper states that generalisation to unseen cards and new card sets is untested. Training-time cheating on hidden information applies here too.

### Learning With Generalised Card Representations for "Magic: The Gathering" (2024)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05879 (full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2407.05879v1) · **Venue:** IEEE CoG 2024 (DOI 10.1109/cog60054.2024.10645602) · **Cited by:** 4 (OpenAlex W4401943577) · **Read:** full text
- **Problem:** Predict human draft and deck choices on MTG cards that are absent from training, by building card representations that transfer.
- **Method:** Card encoder (4 FC layers of 1024, out 512) and deck encoder (6 conv layers plus FC 512) in a Siamese network with triplet loss (margin 1, Euclidean) under contextual preference ranking. Compared inputs: one-hot, random, image-autoencoder latent (32 or 1024-d), 17Lands usage meta (16-d), numerical and categorical features plus a 1306-d sentence-transformer embedding of card text, and combinations.
- **Assumes:** Supervised, offline, no RL, no search. Human picks from 14 MTG sets (about 100M picks, 2,990 unique cards in pre-training, about 75M samples). NEO-only experiments use about 5M samples and 302 cards. Discrete choice with top-1 accuracy.
- **Claims:** NEO-only training, unseen-set average top-1: one-hot NaN; random 23.79%; Meta 42.14%; Features 33.57%; Features+Meta+Image1024 42.87% (NEO test 68.00%). Pre-trained model on BRO (held out entirely): 55.44% (the abstract's "55%"); with tuning on BRO 62.27%. Random-guess accuracy estimated at about 22% in the text. A "45%" figure seen in a search snippet does not appear in the paper text I read.
- **Evaluation:** Top-1 agreement with the human pick, ranking options by embedding distance to the current deck. Held-out NEO test set, and BRO as the unseen set.
- **Code:** none mentioned in the paper text
- **Fit:** good on the generalisation goal. It shows that text plus numeric features transfer to unseen card sets, and gives a clean held-out-set protocol. Breaks on the play goal: it measures imitation accuracy, not win rate, and has no search or leaf value.

### Evolving Evaluation Functions for Collectible Card Game AI (2022)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.5220/0010806900003116 (SciTePress, ICAART 2022, vol. 3, pp. 253-260) · **Venue:** ICAART 2022 · **Cited by:** 7 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** abstract (full text not retrieved; open-access PDF exists per landing page)
- **Problem:** Evolve feature-based evaluation functions for a CCG, and find what matters in the evolution setup.
- **Method:** Evolutionary search over evaluation-function genomes. Three genome representations: weight vector, binary tree, n-ary tree. Three fitness setups: fixed weak opponent, fixed strong opponent, best individual from the previous population.
- **Assumes:** Legends of Code and Magic (LOCM), a small CCG. Feature-based evaluation, not a learned neural net.
- **Claims:** Abstract states that the genome representation and opponent choice matter; no numbers retrieved. Authors say findings generalise beyond LOCM.
- **Evaluation:** LOCM games against fixed and evolved opponents. Details in the PDF.
- **Code:** none found on the landing page
- **Fit:** partial. A leaf evaluator for a CCG tested by evolution, not supervised regression on outcomes. Unseen cards are not mentioned in the abstract.

### Towards Modern Card Games with Large-Scale Action Spaces Through Action Representation (2022)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.12700 · **Venue:** IEEE CoG 2022, pp. 576-579 (DOI 10.1109/CoG51982.2022.9893589) · **Cited by:** 2 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** abstract
- **Problem:** Axie Infinity has a very large action space that makes standard RL hard.
- **Method:** Hybrid RL framework that learns action representations together with strategy. The agent scores a fixed-size subset of candidate actions chosen using the learned representations, not the full action set.
- **Assumes:** Axie Infinity (a card battle game); large discrete action space; RL.
- **Claims:** Abstract only: best overall win rate and best sample efficiency of the three compared methods. No numbers on the abstract page.
- **Evaluation:** Not retrieved (full text at arxiv.org/html/2206.12700v2).
- **Code:** none found
- **Fit:** partial. The idea of scoring a small, representation-selected set of candidate plays fits the afterstate-candidate setting. It does not address unseen cards.

### Causal Reinforcement Learning for Complex Card Games: A Magic The Gathering Benchmark (2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06066 · **Venue:** arXiv preprint (submitted 7 May 2026; cs.LG, cross-listed cs.AI) · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** abstract
- **Problem:** A causal RL benchmark for MTG, with a reference causal agent.
- **Method:** MTG-Causal-RL, a Gymnasium environment with a structural causal model over strategic variables. Reference agent CGFA-PPO uses SCM parents of win probability as factor-aligned critic targets, with an intervention-calibration loss.
- **Assumes:** Partial observation (3,077-d), masked discrete action space (478 actions), five competitive Standard archetypes.
- **Claims:** Masked PPO and CGFA-PPO reach "competitive in-distribution win rates" and beat a random baseline. Cross-archetype leave-one-out transfer is reported as "transfer gaps" with no numbers on the abstract page.
- **Evaluation:** Leave-one-out cross-archetype transfer, paired seeds, paired-bootstrap confidence intervals, Holm-Bonferroni correction. Baselines include random, heuristic, masked PPO, and a scalar control.
- **Code:** none listed on the abstract page. The abstract says the benchmark and protocol are released.
- **Fit:** partial. Its leave-one-out protocol is a template for held-out-group evaluation, but it holds out archetypes, not individual cards. The page makes no unseen-card claim.

### UrzaGPT: LoRA-Tuned Large Language Models for Card Selection in Collectible Card Games (2025)

- **Problem:** LLM-based drafting picks for MTG, with a claim of fast adaptation to new expansions.
- **Method:** LoRA fine-tuning of an open-weight LLM on annotated draft logs. How card text is given to the model is not stated on the abstract page. The base model is not named.
- **Assumes:** MTG real-time draft decisions. Text-based LLM input.
- **Claims:** Untuned Llama-3-8B "completely unable to draft"; zero-shot GPT-4o 43%; fine-tuned smaller models 66.2% accuracy after 10,000 steps; does not match the domain-specific model. No unseen-card results.

### DraftFM: A Foundation Model for Day-Zero Drafting in Magic: The Gathering (2026)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19568 · **Venue:** arXiv preprint (submitted 20 Aug 2026, cs.LG, cross-listed cs.AI; also SSRN 7257098) · **Cited by:** 0 (OpenAlex) · **Read:** abstract (the fetched page gave method and evaluation summaries; treat as abstract-level)
- **Problem:** Draft a new MTG set before any picks exist, when only the public card list is known.
- **Method:** Each card is a frozen 775-d vector built from its public card record, structured features, and a fixed text embedding. No card IDs, set IDs, or usage statistics are used. A 1.6M-parameter discrete-choice network scores each card in the pack from the drafted pool and draft state. Because cards are described only by public attributes, unseen cards go through the same scoring path.
- **Assumes:** Training on 149M human picks from 29 expansions. Discrete choice at each pick.
- **Claims:** Three held-out expansions, top-1 agreement with human picks: 50.8%, 60.4%, 56.7%. Uniform chance at the opening pick about 7%. Day-zero ranking of an unreleased set published about 36 hours before draftable. The paper does not report a separate unseen-card metric, and defers outcome-based evaluation to a follow-up note.
- **Evaluation:** Held-out expansions, human-pick agreement, and agreement with six expert reviewers on the forecast.
- **Code:** github.com/brianward92/mtga (code), github.com/brianward92/draftfm (sealed forecast, tag draftfm-v1.0), Hugging Face weights and frozen eval data. Licence not checked.
- **Fit:** good on the representation idea. Cards built only from public attributes (text, structure) let unseen cards be scored the same way, which matches the goal. Partial on evaluation: it measures pick imitation, not play quality. Single-author 2026 preprint, not peer-reviewed.
